What does Mugarri mean?

Mugarri — the stone that marks the boundary

Mugarri is a Basque compound word: muga (boundary, border) + harri (stone). Literally, the stone that marks the boundary. For centuries the mugarri has marked the edges of municipalities, valleys, common woodland and grazing land across the Basque Country and Navarre: carved stones driven into mountain passes, ridgelines and crossroads by people who knew the ground inch by inch. Many are still standing, and our routes run right past them.

In Basque, mugarri carries a second, figurative meaning: a milestone — a reference point that marks a before and an after.

Why is this place called Mugarri?

Because we work on the boundaries. Our gravel and trail routes cross the borders between Álava, Navarre, Gipuzkoa and Bizkaia, over the same passes and ridgelines where those stones still stand.

Because a mugarri is placed by someone who knows the ground. It is not a catalogue signpost: it is a mark set by hand, in the exact spot, by local people. Routes only locals know how to trace.

And because we want every experience to be a milestone, not a repeated product. Custom, tailored, con mimo. No two routes are the same.
